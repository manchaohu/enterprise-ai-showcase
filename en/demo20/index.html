01｜The problem to solve

Video models process a sequence of frames, not just one photograph. Reversing frame order while preserving decoded pixels provides a controlled model-output contrast, but not proof of temporal reasoning or accurate human action recognition.

02｜How the technology works

Sixteen frames are decoded from an actual H.264 MP4 and passed through the Kinetics-400-finetuned MCG-NJU VideoMAE processor and model on CPU. Tensor shape is 1×16×3×224×224 with 400 action logits. Forward and reverse temporal orders are run separately.

03｜Actual experiment inputs

Pillow creates 16 synthetic moving-red-ball frames, FFmpeg encodes an 8-FPS MP4, and the pipeline reopens and inspects its pixels. Both real model passes retain all 400 logits, ordered hashes and Top-5 labels.

04｜Observed result and independent evidence

Forward inference took 4.241 s and reverse inference 4.206 s on the recorded CPU run; maximum raw logit difference was 0.372211. Top-1 remained 'juggling balls' in both orders despite the scene lacking genuine labeled human action.

06｜What cannot be concluded

The VideoMAE checkpoint carries CC BY-NC-4.0 noncommercial terms. Different logits under frame reversal do not establish temporal reasoning, real-video action accuracy or production reliability.
