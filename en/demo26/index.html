1. Requirements and acceptance

Given three pre-segmented speech clips (A, B and another from A), compute pretrained SpeechBrain X-vectors and compare the third clip with the two references by cosine similarity. The predicted speaker ID comes from the higher similarity. Turn boundaries are known beforehand; blind diarization is not performed.

2. Technical design and data flow

Three public SpeechBrain clips are fetched and hashed without redistributing source audio in the artifact. Audio becomes mono 16kHz and is capped at six seconds. A pretrained EncoderClassifier creates speaker vectors, normalized for cosine comparison. The final timeline uses known clip boundaries plus the predicted identity for the third clip.

3. Core code and implementation details

3.1 Normalize speaker vectors

Source：diarize_e2e.py — Selected excerpt, surrounding context omitted; not standalone executable

v = classifier.encode_batch(wave.unsqueeze(0)).detach().cpu().numpy()
embeddings.append(unit(v))
# unit(): vector / np.linalg.norm(vector)
sims = [cosine(embeddings[2], embeddings[j]) for j in (0,1)]
predicted = 0 if sims[0] >= sims[1] else 1

X-vectors represent speaker-related acoustics, not authenticated human identity. Unit vectors make cosine comparison independent of magnitude. The known third-clip label is only evaluated afterward; it does not force predicted. Recording/channel mismatch can strongly affect similarity.

3.2 Build known-turn timeline

dur = int(wave.numel())
label = [0,1,predicted][i]
segments.append({"start_s": audio_start/SR,
                 "end_s":(audio_start+dur)/SR,
                 "speaker_id":f"SPEAKER_{label}"})
audio_start += dur

Timestamps are computed from concatenated known clip durations, not detected by VAD. Full diarization additionally needs segmentation, overlap handling, speaker-count estimation, clustering and DER evaluation. The metric name explicitly says known turns.

4. Observed results and evidence

Independent verification reports three correct known turns (accuracy 1.0), with the third assigned SPEAKER_0. Cosine scores are stored and independently recomputed. Original third-party WAV recordings are not published; online visuals show derived scores and known-turn timing only.

5. Integration and operational use

Use for controlled speaker-vector similarity on presegmented audio. Meeting diarization needs VAD, segmentation, clustering thresholds, overlap handling and speaker privacy controls. This is not biometric authentication or employee identification.

6. Limits of validation

Three known turns; no blind segmentation, overlap handling, multilingual robustness, channel mismatch evaluation or identity verification. Source audio rights require separate assessment.
